I set myself on the task of rebuilding my trek al domane 2 road bike. I bought my bike in late 2020. Back then I was only 19 and I was even more broke than today. I was only able to buy it because I had earned some money from re-building my parents kitchen at their place. 
Although, I try not to clinch to material things in general, this bike has a high emotional meaning to me. It represents several things: the product of my work and ability to learn something by my own, my means of transport for 5 years to uni and even until today, the sport that pulled me away from bad habits and gives me happy moments surrounded by the people I call friends today, the vehicle of countless trips with my best friend in Uni (no matter how far or close). 

![](static/images/posts/cycling/maintenance/IMG_2218.jpeg)
![](static/images/posts/cycling/maintenance/IMG_0729.jpeg)


I want to share on this post how I did the maintenance of my bike in my flat and the tools I used to do so. Hope someone out there has had the same idea and can learn something from my experience.

Okay, so where to start right? there are so many components in a bike: headsets, bottom brackets, rear&front derailures, hubs, spokes, gears, casettes, brakes.... man and thats just the start each one of this has evolved along the years and has an endless rabithole to fall in.

I was used to take my bike to maintenance every other month (it was way cheaper in Colombia). However, since I live in Germany I haven't been able to service it and you can see the rust and wear on the metal components when you come out of winter (yes I don't stop commuting by bike in winter).



![left](static/images/posts/cycling/maintenance/IMG_0741.jpeg)

I took it all apart, even the wheels. I learned by force lets say how to true bike wheels. Take out a bottom bracket, servicing hubs and changing headsets. I say by force because I happen to do it by the end of March just when the weather is improving and you want to cycle for real. However, a maintenance was required after that long winter.
I started with the brakes, then the gears cables, took out the tires, cut every spoke, released the handle bar, unscrew the crank set and washed the frame and every other component in my bathtub. This picture to the left is almost a complete naked frame. Taken the bottom bracket is quite a struggle, as I would come to learn later.

What I had in my toolbox was this:
- [DURATECH Bicycle Repair Kit 41 Pieces with Tool Box](https://www.amazon.de/dp/B0BHQ4KLF7?ref=ppx_yo2ov_dt_b_fed_asin_title&th=1) Don't buy this.
- Set of spanners 4 - 32
- Set of screwdrivers
- A really cheap truing stand

The first one is cheap and although you get the job done you just feel that its gonna break any moment, wouldn't recommend to anyone to buy this.

One early learning that might be obvious to y'all, you use a lot of consumables:
- Degreaser - I went with an eco friendly one that even said was vegan.
- WD-40
- Grease
- Cleaner - Soap
